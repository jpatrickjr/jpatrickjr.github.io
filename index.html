About

The throughline.

I learned early in the Navy to look beyond the immediate problem and understand the system around it. That instinct followed me into service design, where I became interested not only in what people experience, but in the operations, relationships, and decisions creating that experience.

Over time, understanding the system naturally led to building. I wanted to carry the work further — from discovery and design into products that could be tested in the real world.

AI has expanded what I can prototype, build, and test directly, shortening the distance between an idea and something people can actually use. The throughline is still the same: understand how complicated things work, then make them work better for the people inside them.

Craft

How I work.

Discover → Design → Build → Deploy → Learn

I

Research feeds the build.

Research isn't where the work ends. What I learn shapes what gets built, what gets deployed, and what I learn next.

II

Don't hand off. Ship it.

Most designers hand off a recommendation and walk away. I architect and build the tools the research calls for. StoryGoggles wasn't a brief — it was a problem I saw and solved.

III

Systems, not screens.

Good service design accounts for every touchpoint — digital, human, operational. I work from the frontline user back to the executive suite, because the experience is the whole system, not just the interface.
